Note on experimental resources

Links in green go to PDFs that aren't human-readable.

The way that you should use them is to get a source of more practice to guide your studying, by making LLMs generate more specific practice problems for you.

If you are one of my coachees, doing this extra practice is a great way to prepare for solving hard problems during coaching, and if you can email me your chatlog I can better help guide your studying.

My hope is that if we start to use LLM tools only for preparation, and leave the real tests on pen and paper (treat coaching sessions like tests), then we will be less reliant on them to do our thinking.

Like everything else using LLMs, this is a work in progress.

These are my own creations, and are not official Math 1200 resources.

Please reach out if you have questions or suggestions, and/or come to Office Hours.

To use the LLM study guides

  1. Turn off the new autocomplete (in ChatGPT settings it's Settings → General → Composer and turn off Show predictions).

    [If left on, it will spoil the answers to practice questions. It will also possibly spoil your ability to think for yourself.]

  2. Paste in the document with a prompt like the one below, edited to suit your taste:

You are going to help run a math study session following the above practice document.

Please start by explaining what the student is expected to do and that they should try to do the questions on their own but can request help or skip.

Then, give questions in groups of two or three, always staying within a bullet point or a section.

If the student tries but gets the question wrong, ask how they did it. Unless the error was minor computational, give another practice question.

Similarly, if they ask for a hint or an explanation, give it and then give another practice question.

If they completely don't know how to do it, try to ask a simpler or conceptual question and build

No more than two practice questions of the same kind in one pass, feel free to mark it and move on.

Keep a record of what the student is fluently able to do, able to do independently, or needs more work on (do not say this out loud in the chat, just keep it in memory until the end of the review session, when you can say what the student needs to work more on)
